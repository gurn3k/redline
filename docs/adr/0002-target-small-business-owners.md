# 2. Target small business owners, not freelancers or renters

## Decision

Redline v1 is built for small business owners deciding whether to sign a
contract, not freelancers or renters. Both are turned away in v1, not
deferred.

## Alternatives

- Freelancers — real financial stakes (62% of surveyed NY freelancers lost
  wages to nonpayment), but the strongest evidence describes post-signing
  nonpayment enforcement, not pre-signing comprehension of a risky clause.
- Renters — plausible pain (one of only two verified first-person "I didn't
  understand what I signed" quotes in the research is a lease case), but no
  willingness-to-pay data was found for pre-signing lease review
  specifically.
- Job seekers reviewing job offers — the *other* verified first-person
  quote (the non-compete case) describes exactly this scenario, and it's
  arguably the cleanest pre-signing moment of any segment. Rejected because
  the research found zero willingness-to-pay data for this segment, it's a
  low-frequency (few-times-per-career) need rather than a recurring one,
  and the counter-offer feature assumes negotiating leverage the quote
  itself shows job seekers often don't have ("had no time and had to
  secure the job").

## Why

Small business owners are the only segment with a quantified willingness to
pay (~$153/hour) and a named top-priority need (50% of small businesses with
unmet legal needs cited contract review/negotiation as their top category).
This is a bet on ability and willingness to pay over strength of direct
"didn't see it coming" evidence: the two verified first-person pain quotes
in the research describe an individual consumer and an employee, neither a
business owner.

## Consequences

- Clause prioritization, document types, and pricing should be shaped
  around what a small business owner actually signs (vendor contracts,
  commercial leases, personal guarantees), not consumer ToS or gig-platform
  terms.
