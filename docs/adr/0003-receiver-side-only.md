# 3. Review incoming paper only, not outgoing

## Decision

Redline v1 reviews contracts presented to the User for signature (incoming
paper). It does not review contracts the User drafts or issues to someone
else (outgoing paper).

## Alternatives

- Reviewing outgoing paper too, or instead — arguably an easier build (no
  adversarial party, no risk of misjudging someone else's language, lower
  citation-trust stakes since the User already knows what they wrote) and a
  lower-risk way to earn trust with this User before tackling the harder
  receiver-side problem.

## Why

Every piece of hard evidence in the research (non-competes, arbitration
waivers, auto-renewal traps) describes someone on the receiving end of
boilerplate they didn't write and can't easily negotiate. Choosing
receiver-only means deliberately taking on the harder, higher-stakes problem
— citation-grounded claims about someone else's document under negotiation
pressure — over the easier, lower-risk build that outgoing-paper review
would have been.

## Consequences

- A small business owner's real contract exposure is roughly half incoming,
  half outgoing. Redline only covers one side of that in v1.
- The citation-grounding bar (ADR 0001) matters more, not less, because the
  document was written by an adversarial party rather than the User.
